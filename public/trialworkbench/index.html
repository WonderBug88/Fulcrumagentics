Why AI alone is not enough

A chatbot may make your story feel stronger. A judge asks what you can prove.

Most people paste facts into AI for reassurance. Trial Workbench is more specific: it organizes the record, tests the elements, studies the law, and shows where your case is strong, weak, or missing proof.

Validation is not preparation

AI can echo your theory back to you. Trial Workbench challenges it with evidence, opposing arguments, procedural issues, and missing facts.

Context gets fragmented

One chat becomes ten chats, then thirty. Trial Workbench keeps the dispute in one semantic case memory instead of scattered conversations.

The court needs proof

A strong story still needs admissible exhibits, chronology, legal elements, case law, damages, and a clean package an attorney or court can review.

The Case Spine

Your whole dispute organized around what happened, what matters, and what proves it.

The Case Spine is the backbone of Trial Workbench. It connects people, dates, documents, claims, defenses, damages, and legal research into one living structure that can be reviewed, improved, and handed to an attorney.

Chronology

A timeline of key events tied to the documents that support them.

Evidence Map

A clear index of exhibits, facts, sources, and where each item fits.

Legal Elements

Claims mapped against the elements that must be proven.

Gaps and Weaknesses

Missing facts, damages issues, defenses, and likely attacks are surfaced early.

Case Law Notes

Relevant cases summarized by issue, outcome, and why they matter.

Attorney Handoff

A clean package that helps a lawyer understand the matter faster.

Litigation Intelligence

Study the law, the filings, and how similar fights actually played out.

Trial Workbench is designed around legal reality: what the law says, how courts have interpreted it, how parties have been sued, what arguments worked, what failed, and what evidence moved the case.

CourtListener research

Use opinions and case law to understand how judges discuss similar claims, standards, defenses, and procedural issues.

PACER-style filing review

Review complaints, motions, orders, and litigation history so users can learn from real case paths instead of theory alone.

Opposing counsel lens

The system does not just cheerlead. It looks for counterarguments, missing proof, procedural risk, and weaknesses before the other side does.
